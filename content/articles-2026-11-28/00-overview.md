## 1. Research synthesis — key findings per domain

### Sales Coaching
- There is **no 2024–2026 primary research isolating coaching's causal effect on SDR performance specifically**. The honest evidence base is: large surveys on the coaching *gap* (Salesforce 2026, Ambition 2026, Mindtickle 2025–26), SDR structural benchmarks (Bridge Group 2025), and older correlational studies (CSO Insights 2019, Korn Ferry 2023, CEB 2011) that still power most "coaching lifts X%" claims in circulation — usually re-dated by vendor blogs.
- The pattern across the new research: organisations *have* coaching frameworks (77%, Ambition 2026) but don't run them consistently (38% report consistent adoption); only 30% coach weekly or bi-weekly; 46% of reps rarely get feedback on their actual sales conversations (Salesforce 2026).
- The dominant practitioner tensions: 1:1s hijacked by pipeline inspection; deal coaching (rescues the quarter) vs skill coaching (compounds); AI call-scoring as coaching tool vs surveillance; frequency without manager skill can backfire (CEB 2011: quality beats quantity; gains concentrate in the middle 60% of reps).

### Sales Enablement & SDR ramp
- One dataset does most of the legitimate work in this field: **The Bridge Group 2025** (351 B2B companies). Its headline tension drives Article 2: SDR ramp is the *fastest ever recorded* (3.0 months, lowest since 2010) while SDR quota attainment is the *worst ever recorded* (60%, lowest in study history). Speed and success are moving in opposite directions.
- Quotas themselves have fallen ~40% since 2018 (median 10 held meetings/month) and attainment still fell — hard to explain as a rep-quality problem alone.
- Practitioner consensus: staged ramp with readiness gates beats time-based ramp and content-dump onboarding; enablement measured on activity (sessions delivered, completions) rather than behaviour and outcomes is why the function gets cut first.
- SDR headcount is contracting: 36% of B2B software companies cut SDR/BDR headcount in the prior year, the steepest cut of any sales role (Emergence Capital × Benchmarkit 2025). AI SDRs were 1% of Bridge Group respondents in 2025 — a category that exists, not a takeover.

### Learning & Development measurement
- Four independent bodies (ATD, CIPD, Fosway, Brandon Hall) converge over a decade: ~80% of organisations measure reaction/learning, about half measure behaviour, a third or fewer measure results, and only a small minority do anything resembling ROI — and Fosway's 2025 data says this has **not materially improved**.
- The peer-reviewed anchor: trainee reactions ("happy sheets") correlate with measured learning at **r ≈ .09** (Alliger et al. 1997; Sitzmann et al. 2008) — close to no relationship at all.
- Kirkpatrick isn't the villain; the failure is measuring forwards from Level 1 instead of planning backwards from results, and ignoring transfer conditions (Blume et al. 2010: supervisor support is among the strongest predictors of whether training transfers).

### Go-To-Market & the first conversation
- The strongest independently checkable story: **Gartner (June 2025, n=632 buyers)** — 73% of B2B buyers *actively avoid* suppliers who send irrelevant outreach, and 69% report inconsistencies between a supplier's website and what its sellers say.
- **6sense (2025 Buyer Experience Report):** 94% of buying groups had ranked vendors before first seller contact; 95% of eventual winners were on the Day One shortlist. The first conversation is mostly validation — or disqualification.
- Outreach economics are brutal and measurable: Belkins' 2025 data puts average cold-email reply rate at 0.45% of sends; Cognism/WHAM found cold-call conversation-to-meeting success fell to 2.3% in 2025 from 4.82% in 2024; Hunter found 71% of decision-makers cite irrelevance as the top reason for not replying.
- The mechanical loop practitioners name: meetings-booked targets → reps optimise for acceptance not fit → buyers get irrelevant outreach → buyers avoid the *supplier* → reply rates fall → leadership raises activity targets (112 activities/day median, Bridge Group) → repeat.

## 3. Deliberately excluded — famous stats that did not survive verification

These appear in competitor content everywhere. We are not using them, and several articles say so in passing — which is itself a credibility play for both human readers and AI citation.

- **"Reps forget 70% of training in 24 hours / 90% in a week"** — a misreading of Ebbinghaus (1885), who tested himself on nonsense syllables and measured relearning "savings", not recall of real training. The popular percentages appear in neither his work nor the 2015 replication.
- **"Only 10% of training transfers"** — traces to a 1982 opinion-article estimate with no data behind it; named "the 10% delusion" by Ford, Yelon & Billington (2011) after 35+ uncritical repetitions.
- **"70:20:10 is a research-based ratio"** — the ratio was rounded "for memorability" by its own popularisers from retrospective executive recollections; the underlying 1988 study contains no such ratio and measured no learning.
- **"Enablement produces a 49% higher win rate"** — a misreading of CSO Insights 2019: the win rate *was* 49.0% (vs 42.5% without enablement), a 6.5-point gap, not +49%.
- **"Dynamic coaching improves win rates by 32.1% — CSO Insights 2025"** — no such 2025 report exists; the figure is a 2019 relative gap re-dated by roundup sites. Same story for "73% of managers spend <5% of their time coaching (CSO Insights 2024)" and "94% vs 58% quota attainment" coaching claims — no primary source found.
- **"Sales training delivers 353% ROI" / "$4 back for every $1 of enablement" / "strong onboarding = 82% higher retention, 70% more productivity"** — vendor-roundup chains with no traceable primary study.
- **AI SDR vendor performance claims** ("8–14% reply rates", "cost per meeting $80 vs $600") — aggregator marketing attributed to reports that could not be located.
- **"Buyers are 57%/70% through the journey before talking to sales" (as a current stat)** — a single 2011 CEB/Google survey, never re-fielded on the same basis. The current repeated measure (6sense) is 61% and moving the other way.

## 6. Landscape: MySalesCoach and the SDR Leaders communities (added 28 Nov 2026, Nick's request)

### MySalesCoach — peer/competitor content benchmark (for Article 1 especially)

**What they are:** UK company (Newcastle upon Tyne), founded 2022; seed-funded (£800k, Northstar Ventures, Nov 2023). Originally expert-matched 1:1 coaching (a bench of 80+ coaches across SDR, AE, enterprise, leadership and CS roles); in September 2026 they launched the **"Sales Coaching Operating System"** — an AI agent that flags coachable moments in the flow of work, per-rep growth plans built from a diagnostic across 20+ competencies, daily live group sessions, and a pool of 1:1 coaching time.

**Their content engine:** proprietary research as the lead magnet — *The State of Sales Coaching* (2025 edition produced with Aircall, 1,600+ respondents; a 2026 edition followed) and *The Modern Revenue Leader's Sales Coaching Manual* (March 2026). Their statistics page is built to be quoted (and is — it surfaced repeatedly in our research). Their product pages are FAQ-dense and aimed squarely at buyer questions ("how often does each rep get coached?", "how do you measure impact?") — the same intent Article 1 targets.

**Where they're strong:** the frequency argument, owned data, category language ("operating system"), AI + human framing, scale (any team size, any region).

**Where they're thin — and where EXO is genuinely different:**
- They sell coaching *delivered by their coaches* — a rented bench. EXO builds the capability *inside* the client: the manager learns to coach, the system stays when the engagement ends. Article 1's "nobody trained the managers" section is aimed exactly at this gap, without naming them.
- They are role-general (SDR, AE, CSM, leaders). EXO is sales-development specific: onboarding, ramp and coaching as **one system**, not coaching as a standalone subscription.
- Their content is product-led; there is no buyer-experience philosophy underneath it. EXO's Article 5 position (craft, the buyer's experience, AI as amplifier) has no equivalent in their material.
- Their research is vendor research, like Ambition's and Mindtickle's — usable, attributed, and labelled as such in our stat bank. We cite their 76%/47% frequency finding because it's the best current version of it, and we say who produced it. That's the honest way to share a stage with them: out-evidence, don't out-shout.

**Rule for future content:** benchmark against MySalesCoach's topics and formats; never borrow their wording, category language or frameworks.

## 7. A note on GEO (the AI equivalent of SEO)

Each article is built to be cited, not just ranked: a quotable 2–3 sentence answer summary up top, question-led headings that match how people phrase questions to AI assistants, named sources with dates attached to every statistic (AI engines preferentially cite attributed claims), short declarative paragraphs, and FAQ sections with direct answers. The deliberate exclusion of folklore stats is part of the strategy — when an AI engine cross-checks, these articles are the ones still standing.
