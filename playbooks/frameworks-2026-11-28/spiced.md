# SPICED — Research Brief & EXO Fit

**EXO Enablement — internal framework file. Researched 28 November 2026.**

**One-line verdict:** EXO's first-call discovery and handover language. SPICED runs the conversation; MEDDPICC inspects the deal. Of all the sales frameworks here, this is the closest native fit to SDR work — taught as a way of listening, never as a CRM form.

**Evidence status:** Practitioner diagnostic framework from a commercial consultancy (Winning by Design), widely adopted in B2B SaaS / recurring-revenue teams, with a coherent retention rationale and a large practice base claimed by its originator. **No independent, peer-reviewed empirical validation.** Vendor origin must be disclosed whenever it is taught as authoritative. Strongest honest claim: a well-designed shared language for diagnosis and handover across SDR → AE → CS — not a validated predictor of deal outcomes.

## Part 1 — Research brief

### Origin & source

**Winning by Design (WbD)**, a global B2B revenue consulting and training firm founded in **2012** by **Jacco van der Kooij** (founding confirmed in BusinessWire, 2 Apr 2024; Crunchbase, accessed Nov 2026).

**When was SPICED itself created? Stated honestly:** no single definitive original publication date is consistently cited. Umbrex (*SPICED Framework*, Sep 2026): "Origin: Winning by Design; in use since at least the late 2010s." Some secondary sources conflate the firm's 2012 founding with the framework's. The canonical, precisely datable source is WbD's own blueprint: *The SPICED Framework* (Blueprint, 15 Apr 2022, winningbydesign.com). SPICED also sits inside WbD's wider work — *Blueprints for a SaaS Sales Organization* (van der Kooij & Pizarro, 2018) and *Revenue Architecture* (van der Kooij, 2023), where it is a core element of the "Bowtie" model of the full customer journey.

**Intellectual stance:** WbD positions SPICED against seller-centric qualification. The founding analogy is medical: diagnose before prescribing. WbD's recurring-revenue thesis — "recurring revenue comes from recurring impact" — is why SPICED was built for SaaS/subscription businesses: if the customer doesn't keep realising the Impact diagnosed at sale, they don't renew.

**Commercial interest, disclosed:** WbD sells consulting, training and its Revenue Academy built around its own frameworks. Every primary source for SPICED is a WbD publication; adoption claims ("1,000+ SaaS companies") originate from the seller. That doesn't make it wrong. It does set the weight its claims can carry.

### What it actually is

Five elements across six letters (C+E together form one element, Critical Event). WbD's definitions, from the SPICED Blueprint (Apr 2022):

| Letters | Element | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| **S** | **Situation** | Facts and background only: team, tools, process, landscape. Mostly researchable before the call — confirm, don't interrogate. No opinions. |
| **P** | **Pain** | The specific challenge that brought the prospect your way — concrete, observable, in the buyer's words, with a recent example. Both quantifiable pain and qualitative pain (who is frustrated, and why). |
| **I** | **Impact** | The business consequence of solving — or not solving — the Pain. WbD groups rational impact into: increase revenue, decrease cost, improve customer experience — plus personal/emotional stakes. Evidence standard: a number or outcome the *buyer* accepts, not the seller's projection. This element later becomes the renewal test. |
| **CE** | **Critical Event** | The deadline to achieve that impact — a date/milestone/external force with a consequence if missed (renewal, cohort start, regulatory deadline, board date). Not the seller's quarter-end. No Critical Event usually means no real timeline. WbD notes Impact and Critical Event may surface on the very first SDR call, or only over several meetings. |
| **D** | **Decision** | The process, committee and criteria involved in purchasing: who judges, against what (must-haves vs nice-to-haves), what steps and approvals sit between now and signature, who could stop it. One element deliberately bundling what MEDDPICC splits into three. |

**How WbD says to use it — the defining feature:**

> "Use SPICED as a framework to gather information on calls, for hand-offs between teams (e.g., prospecting to sales, sales to customer success), or for summarizing deals during internal forecasting meetings." — Winning by Design, *SPICED Blueprint*, Apr 2022

SPICED is designed to **travel across the Bowtie**: the Situation and Pain an SDR captures should be the same record the AE deepens and Customer Success inherits at onboarding.

**Diagnostic vs qualification — the distinction, precisely:** independent guides converge with WbD's own language. "SPICED runs the call. MEDDPICC qualifies the deal." (Craig Irwin, 2026.) "It is not a qualification checklist scored after the call... SPICED is the conversation itself, closer to a doctor's exam than an audit." (PitchMonster, Nov 2026.) SPICED *can* disqualify (a missing Critical Event is a strong signal), but its primary job is diagnosis and shared understanding. Treating its fields as a post-call scorecard is "the single biggest implementation mistake" (Prospeo, 2026).

### Evidence base — including criticisms, stated plainly

**What supports it:** coherence with its problem (a sale never properly diagnosed churns — structuring discovery around Impact and carrying it into onboarding is logically aligned with retention economics); a large claimed practice base (WbD: work with "over 1,000 SaaS companies", BusinessWire, Apr 2024 — a practice base, not a controlled study); underlying ingredients (structured discovery, buyer-articulated impact, adaptive questioning) are consistent with the peer-reviewed predictors of sales performance in Verbeke et al. (2011) — convergent support for the ingredients, not validation of the package.

**What does not support it:**

1. **Vendor origin.** All primary sources are WbD publications; prominence is inseparable from WbD's marketing. Claims in some round-ups that SPICED is "among the most evidence-backed" frameworks in B2B SaaS cite breadth of deployment, not controlled evidence.
2. **No independent empirical validation.** No peer-reviewed study tests SPICED adherence against win rate, forecast accuracy, retention or expansion. No independently audited outcome dataset exists.
3. **Thin where enterprise deal control matters.** No equivalent of MEDDPICC's Economic Buyer test, Champion test, Paper Process or Competition element. "You're building a pipeline of strong interest that converts at 8% instead of 25%" (Craig Irwin, 2026 — the figures are the author's assertion; the structural point stands). Replicate Labs (Nov 2026): SPICED "thin on: Champion; paper process; competition."
4. **Assumes the stated Pain is the real problem.** Diagnostic Sales (2026): "The main limitation of SPICED is that it assumes the Pain the customer describes is the real problem. In complex B2B decisions, the first pain a buyer names is usually a symptom." SPICED has no explicit root-cause step.
5. **Decision covers process, not confidence.** No element captures buyer confidence, internal political risk or champion strength.
6. **Cost/benefit limit at the low end.** Less useful for one-off, low-value transactions where a five-part diagnosis costs more than the deal is worth; practitioner sweet spot is consultative SaaS (Prospeo, 2026).

**Sources:** Winning by Design, *SPICED Blueprint* (15 Apr 2022) and blueprint page (updated Jan 2026); van der Kooij & Pizarro (2018); van der Kooij, *Revenue Architecture* (2023); BusinessWire (2 Apr 2024); Umbrex (Sep 2026); Rework (Oct 2026); Diagnostic Sales (2026); PitchMonster (Nov 2026); Prospeo (2026); Craig Irwin (2026); Replicate Labs (Nov 2026).

## Part 2 — EXO fit

### How EXO deploys it

SPICED is EXO's **default discovery and handover language for SDR teams**, deployed in layers:

- **Week One — Situation + Pain.** Research the Situation before the call; let the buyer name the Pain in their own words on it. This is EXO's core craft teaching: diagnose before pitching. It is also the buyer's experience of the call — being understood rather than processed — which is the EXO philosophy in a first conversation.
- **Week One → Month Three — test for Critical Event.** A good SDR can legitimately ask "Is there a date this has to be solved by? What happens if it slips?" In EXO's buyers' world: a new SDR cohort start date, a headcount plan, a board pipeline target. If there is none, recording that honestly is better qualification than manufacturing urgency.
- **Month Three — the SPICED handover.** "Write a handover the AE thanks you for" is a concrete, assessable Month Three skill. The handover note carries Situation, Pain in the buyer's words, any Impact/Critical Event surfaced, and Decision unknowns *flagged as unknown*. The AE continues a story instead of restarting one; CS inherits the same record — WbD's Bowtie logic, applied to EXO's clients.
- **Manager coaching.** Managers review calls against SPICED as a *thinking* check — did we actually diagnose, or did we pitch at a symptom? — which connects directly to SPICED's known weakness (stated Pain ≠ real problem). EXO coaches one level deeper than the framework goes: "That's the pain they named. What's underneath it?"

### When it fits / when it doesn't

- **Fits:** recurring-revenue and consultative B2B; any team where SDR → AE → CS continuity matters; discovery coaching at every stage.
- **Doesn't fit, alone:** enterprise deal control. SPICED has no Champion, Economic Buyer, Paper Process or Competition lens. EXO's hybrid is the practitioner consensus, not a compromise: **SPICED to run the conversation, MEDDPICC to inspect the deal afterwards.**
- **Doesn't fit at all:** as five mandatory CRM fields per call. That reproduces the checkbox failure mode every independent guide warns about and contradicts EXO's craft-over-volume positioning.

### Where it lives in the Learning Design Playbook

- **Step 1 (Diagnose the gap):** SPICED is itself a diagnostic discipline, and it mirrors the playbook's rule — diagnose before you design / diagnose before you pitch. A rep who "can't do discovery" is worked through the six gap types before SPICED is re-taught; often the gap is habit or environment (a sequencer that rewards speed), not knowledge of the framework.
- **Part 2, rule 14 (practise the actual task):** SPICED is taught through real calls and real accounts, with feedback against whether the *buyer's* words were captured — the playbook's rubric principle (rule 17) applied to the handover note as the assessable artefact.
- **Part 3 (e-learning):** Situation research and Pain-spotting from real call recordings are good fast-layer module material; the diagnosis conversation itself is a slow skill, built in live practice — the playbook's pace-layering rule (Step 3) decides which parts go where.

**Tension flagged:** WbD's opportunity matrix extends beyond the five letters (Status Quo, Competitors, Next Steps). EXO teaches the five as the conversation core and borrows the MEDDPICC lenses for the rest, rather than quietly expanding SPICED into a second checklist. And EXO discloses the source when teaching it — "This comes from Winning by Design, a consultancy that sells training in it. It's widely used in SaaS, it hasn't been independently validated, and here's why we use the parts we use." That honesty is the brand.
