# MEDDICC / MEDDPICC — Research Brief & EXO Fit

**EXO Enablement — internal framework file. Researched 28 November 2026.**

**One-line verdict:** The inspection language for complex, multi-stakeholder deals — and mostly an AE tool. EXO uses a stripped "known vs assumed" discipline for SDR handovers and full MEDDPICC only for manager coaching, promotion readiness, and EXO's own enterprise deals.

**Evidence status:** Practitioner framework, widely adopted for complex enterprise B2B qualification over ~30 years, reverse-engineered from one company's deal reviews. No independent, peer-reviewed empirical validation that using it causes higher win rates or forecast accuracy. Vendor and trainer claims are testimonial and correlational. Strongest honest claim: a proven inspection language for large, multi-stakeholder deals — not a validated performance intervention.

## Part 1 — Research brief

### Origin & source

Created in **1996** inside **PTC** (Parametric Technology Corporation, the CAD/engineering software company selling Pro/ENGINEER into large manufacturers), credited to **Dick Dunkel** working alongside **Jack Napoli**, under SVP of Sales **John McMahon**:

> "MEDDIC was created in 1996 inside PTC by Dick Dunkel, working under SVP John McMahon alongside teammate Jack Napoli." — MEDDICC Ltd, *Who Created MEDDIC?*, meddicc.com (19 Mar 2025, updated 9 Nov 2026)

The trigger was scale: at roughly 300 reps, attrition threatened growth. Dunkel and Napoli studied past and present opportunities — why did PTC win, lose, or see deals slip? — and the recurring pattern became the six elements.

**Verification note:** accounts differ in detail, and most surviving accounts come from people or firms who sell MEDDIC training. McMahon told Sprinklr (2021) he had "always taught specific items as qualification parameters" before the acronym existed and that Napoli tied the acronym to them. Darius Lahoutifard (ran PTC France, later founded MEDDIC Academy) describes the practices growing up among PTC's regional leaders before HQ compiled and named them. What all accounts agree on: PTC, mid-1990s, reverse-engineered from won/lost/slipped deal reviews, intended as a common language for forecast inspection. **Not** the product of a published research programme.

Spread came via the PTC alumni diaspora. McMahon became a five-time CRO and wrote *The Qualified Sales Leader* (2021). Andy Whyte's *MEDDICC* (25 Nov 2020), with foreword/commentary from Dunkel and Napoli, is the first book-length codification.

**Folklore check:** MEDDICC Ltd credits PTC with going "$0 to $1 billion in ten years" and credits "much of that growth" to MEDDIC discipline; other retellings give different windows ("$300M to $1B in four years"). Treat these as vendor-credited claims about PTC's overall growth, not audited evidence MEDDIC caused it. No source separates MEDDIC's effect from product, market and hiring effects.

**Trademark note:** in April 2026 a US federal court (E.D. Pa.) ruled MEDDPICC a generic term and ordered its US trademark registration cancelled. The acronym family is now generic industry vocabulary, taught by competing firms.

### What it actually is

MEDDIC is **a qualification and deal-inspection framework — not a discovery conversation method and not a full selling methodology**. It defines what a rep and manager need to *know* about a deal before they can honestly forecast it. It does not teach how to run the conversation that uncovers it.

The original six:

| Letter | Element | Evidence standard in practice |
|---|---|---|
| **M** | **Metrics** | The quantified business outcome the buyer expects, in the buyer's own numbers — baseline, target, owner. Not a number from the seller's value model. |
| **E** | **Economic Buyer** | The one person with discretionary authority to release funds — can say yes when others hesitate, no when everyone else says yes. Direct engagement, or their priorities confirmed via the Champion. A budget holder or friendly sponsor is not automatically the EB. |
| **D** | **Decision Criteria** | The technical, commercial and relationship standards the buying group will actually judge against — formal and informal, including criteria you lose on, ideally criteria you helped shape. |
| **D** | **Decision Process** | The sequence of steps, people, meetings, reviews and approvals that produces a *decision*. Distinct from Paper Process, which starts after it. |
| **I** | **Identify Pain** (now often "Implicate the Pain") | The business problem forcing change and its consequence if left alone — connected to the Metrics and the Economic Buyer's priorities, not just a named complaint. |
| **C** | **Champion** | An internal advocate who sells for you when you're not in the room. MEDDICC Ltd's three tests: has power and influence; acts as an internal seller; has a vested/personal interest. A contact who only gives information is a **Coach** — useful, not forecastable. Champions are tested by costly actions: arranging the EB meeting, co-authoring the business case. |

The additions:

- **MEDDICC** adds **C — Competition**: named rivals, internal build, another project competing for the same budget, and most commonly the real rival — doing nothing.
- **MEDDPICC** adds **P — Paper Process** (after Decision Process): procurement, legal redlines, security review, DPA, vendor onboarding, PO, signature authority — everything between a verbal yes and a signed contract, with owners and durations. (Attribution of the P is contested; its function is not.)

Full MEDDPICC order (MEDDICC Ltd): Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Paper Process, Implicate the Pain, Champion, Competition.

**How it's used:** as a scorecard/deal-review audit. For each letter: do we *know* this, with evidence from the buyer, or are we assuming it? Gaps become next actions. The letters are not a chronological script — Whyte himself warns against junior reps feeling they "need to check off answers to every element during every engagement" (*MEDDICC*, 2020).

### Evidence base — including criticisms, stated plainly

**What supports it:** ~30 years of continuous use in complex enterprise software, spread by operator diaspora rather than marketing alone; strong face validity as an inspection checklist (independent comparison Scian, May 2026, rates it 5/5 for qualification rigour and enterprise fit); it solves a real observed failure pattern — deals forecast on need and enthusiasm that slip because nobody mapped authority, process or paperwork.

**What does not support it:**

1. **No independent empirical validation.** No peer-reviewed study, controlled comparison, or independently audited dataset tests MEDDIC/MEDDPICC adherence against win rate or forecast accuracy. A 2026 synthesis of the peer-reviewed sales literature (Giggy, drawing on Verbeke, Dietz & Verwaal 2011 and Franke & Park 2006): "None of the four [major commercial methodologies] has been validated by an independent randomised controlled comparison"; what meta-analyses link to performance is *adaptive selling* and *selling-related knowledge*, not adherence to a named methodology.
2. **Built for enterprise, misapplied elsewhere.** PeerLab (*Ranked: Which Sales Methodology Fits Your GTM Motion*, May 2026): "Roll the same eight-letter checklist out to an SMB team doing forty dials a day and you haven't added discipline. You've added a tax on the one resource that motion runs on: talk time." Scian (May 2026) scores rep adoption ease 2/5, SMB/velocity fit 1/5. Practitioner consensus (GigRadar, July 2026): full MEDDPICC overhead exceeds value below roughly $50k ARR.
3. **CRM theatre.** The most common failure is implementation: reps back-fill scorecards after calls to satisfy a pipeline review; "Champion" gets applied to anyone friendly. "MEDDIC is a fine diagnostic. It's a terrible operating system." (Felix Gray, *MEDDIC Is Overrated*, Medium, Apr 2026.) DealManagement.co (Feb 2026) calls the same pattern "field completion theater."
4. **Context shift since 1996.** PTC sold large perpetual licences to buying committees with formal procurement. Much modern B2B is smaller, faster, subscription SaaS — where Paper Process and committee mapping matter least and the full framework is over-engineered.

**Sources:** MEDDICC Ltd, meddicc.com (2025/26); Whyte, *MEDDICC* (2020); McMahon, *The Qualified Sales Leader* (2021); Bebop, *MEDDIC Sales Methodology* (Oct 2026); PeerLab (May 2026); Scian (May 2026); GigRadar (July 2026); DealManagement.co (2026); Giggy synthesis (May 2026) on Verbeke et al. (2011), Franke & Park (2006).

## Part 2 — EXO fit

### How EXO deploys it

MEDDPICC lives in EXO in exactly three places — and is kept out of a fourth:

1. **SDR handover discipline (all stages, light).** The transferable core for SDRs is not the eight letters; it's the *known vs assumed* standard. A handover states what the buyer actually said (evidence), labels what is not yet known (Economic Buyer not identified, criteria not agreed), and never dresses enthusiasm up as qualification. This protects trust inside the team and with the buyer — which is EXO's whole positioning (End-User Experience Orientation starts with the internal customer's experience too).
2. **Champion vs Coach (Week One concept, Month Three skill).** Teaching SDRs early not to mistake a friendly, chatty contact for a Champion stops inflated pipeline at source. By Month Three the test is practical: can this rep tell the difference on their own accounts and say what a Champion would have *done* by now?
3. **Manager coaching and Promotion readiness.** Full MEDDPICC literacy is a manager and pre-AE competency. At the Promotion stage, the EXO assessment is: can this SDR run clean discovery, distinguish coach from champion, and write a handover an AE can forecast from? Managers get the full framework because they inspect deals with it.
4. **EXO's own deals.** When Nick qualifies a large enablement engagement — multiple stakeholders, procurement path, budget owner — MEDDPICC is the inspection tool. That's the context it was built for.

**Kept out of:** SDR stage-gates and activity metrics. MEDDPICC completion is never an SDR target. Eight evidence fields per opportunity on a 40-touch day is the "tax on talk time" failure, and it would contradict EXO's craft-over-volume position.

### When it fits / when it doesn't

- **Fits:** complex deals, multiple stakeholders, long cycles, formal procurement; deal inspection with managers; EXO's own enterprise engagements.
- **Doesn't fit:** high-velocity or transactional motions; early SDR onboarding beyond the three light touches above; any context where filling the fields becomes the work (CRM theatre is the failure mode, and EXO names it as such in coaching).

### Where it lives in the Learning Design Playbook

- **Step 1 (Diagnose the gap):** a team "using MEDDPICC" whose forecasts don't improve is usually an environment/communication gap (fields completed for the review, criteria not shared) — not a knowledge gap. EXO diagnoses before retraining.
- **Step 3 (Set the destination):** MEDDPICC for SDRs is a *familiarisation-to-conscious-action* target on three elements only; full proficiency is a Promotion/manager target. The playbook's honesty rule applies: don't promise full MEDDPICC competence from a workshop.
- **Part 2, rule 17 (one standard everywhere):** where MEDDPICC is used, the same evidence language ("known / assumed / unknown") is used in workshop, coaching and manager reviews — one standard, heard everywhere.

**Tension flagged:** MEDDPICC's inspection rigour can pull coaching conversations toward interrogation of the rep. EXO's style rule resolves it — hard on the deal's evidence, invested in the rep. The framework audits the deal, never the person's worth.
