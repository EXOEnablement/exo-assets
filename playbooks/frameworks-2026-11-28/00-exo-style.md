# The EXO Style — How EXO Coaches

**EXO Enablement — internal positioning document. Set by Nick, 28 November 2026. Not public copy yet.**

Hard on the problem. Invested in the person. Everything below is what that sentence means in practice.

---

## The style, in Nick's words

> Coaches from experience, having carried the number and used MEDDICC, SPICED, BANT and Challenger in live deals. Fits the method to your team and gives direct feedback, including the hard conversations. Frameworks: GROW, Kolb, 70:20:10 and Kirkpatrick.

Developed, that is four commitments:

### 1. Coaches from experience

EXO's coaching comes from having done the job. Carried the number in outbound sales roles — 144% of quota at Person Centred Software, 127% at CourseCo, 350+ meetings booked in a single year — and from coaching 50+ professionals and running 300+ live workshops across five regions since. The methodologies in this library are not things EXO read about. They are things that were used, under quota pressure, in live deals — and, just as importantly, things whose failure modes were met in person. When EXO teaches MEDDPICC, it teaches the CRM-theatre failure too, because that's what actually happens to teams. A coach who has only studied a framework teaches the framework. A coach who has worked under one teaches the framework *and* where it breaks.

### 2. Fits the method to your team

No framework in this library is applied off the shelf. The same SPICED that runs a first call for a Week One SDR is the wrong tool, alone, for a complex enterprise deal; the same MEDDPICC that inspects an enterprise deal is a tax on a high-velocity SDR's talk time. EXO's first discipline is diagnosis — which gap, which stage, which motion — and the method is chosen, cut and sequenced to fit the team in front of us. A provider who arrives with the framework already decided is selling the framework. EXO arrives with questions.

### 3. Direct feedback, including the hard conversations

Feedback is against explicit criteria — the call scorecard, the rubric — never vibes, and it is given in the open, on the work. When a rep's discovery is shallow, when a manager's coaching cadence is a calendar invite and nothing more, when a deal is being forecast on a friendly contact and hope: EXO says it, plainly, with the evidence next to it. Hard on the problem means the hard conversation happens — early, specifically, and with a way forward attached. Invested in the person means it is always about the work, never a verdict on the rep, and never delivered in a way that shames anyone in front of a room. The two halves are not in tension. Being honest with someone about their calls *is* the investment.

### 4. Frameworks are tools, held honestly

EXO uses named frameworks because shared language makes coaching faster and standards visible. EXO also tells the truth about them: what each one is, where it came from, what evidence sits behind it, and where it stops working. Every framework file in this library carries an evidence status, including the uncomfortable ones. That honesty is not a caveat on the offer. It is the offer — buyers have been sold methodology folklore for thirty years, and an SDR leader can smell it.

## The incorporation map — where each framework sits in EXO's method

**Sales methodologies** (what reps sell with):

| Framework | Where it lives in EXO delivery | One-line evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| **SPICED** | The default discovery & handover language. Week One: Situation + Pain on first calls. Month Three: the SPICED handover note. Managers review calls against it. | Practitioner framework from a consultancy (Winning by Design); widely adopted in SaaS; no independent empirical validation. Vendor origin disclosed when taught. |
| **BANT** | Week One triage only — need-led, as the note structure *after* the call, never the script of it. Graduated away from by Month Three. | Unvalidated IBM-era heuristic (1950s–60s, no primary source exists); useful lightweight triage, misleading as a hard gate on complex deals. |
| **Challenger** | Behaviours in miniature from Month Three (point of view in outbound, tailor by persona, control the next step; Mobilizers vs Talkers). Full commercial teaching: Promotion stage / AE readiness, and EXO's own selling. | One large proprietary correlational vendor study (CEB, 2009), never independently replicated, substantively criticised; Gartner moved on to Sense Making in 2019. |
| **MEDDICC / MEDDPICC** | Light touches for SDRs (known-vs-assumed handover discipline; Champion vs Coach). Full framework: manager coaching, Promotion readiness, EXO's own enterprise deals. Never an SDR stage-gate. | Practitioner framework from PTC's 1990s deal reviews; ~30 years of enterprise adoption; no independent validation that it causes higher win rates. |

**Coaching & learning frameworks** (how EXO coaches and designs):

| Framework | Where it lives in EXO delivery | One-line evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| **GROW** | The default structure of EXO coaching conversations — Nick's 1:1s, and the cadence EXO teaches SDR managers. Rep self-assesses first; one committed action out the door. | Practitioner model (Whitmore et al., 1992); no adequate direct empirical testing; general coaching evidence supports coaching, not GROW specifically. |
| **Kolb cycle** | The spine of practice design in the Learning Design Playbook: call → review recording → extract one principle → retry. Labels used in session blueprints to catch a missing stage. | Design heuristic with weak direct evidence as a literal model of learning. (The styles/LSI spin-off: not evidence-based — **not used**, see caution below.) |
| **70:20:10** | Programme-architecture shorthand in client conversation: the floor is the 70, the manager is the 20, formal training is the amplifying 10 — all three designed, none left to chance. Inverted for novices, deliberately. | ⚠ Heuristic, not a validated formula — percentages derive from rounded retrospective self-reports of 191 executives (CCL, 1988/1996). Never quoted as fact; never a budget split. |
| **Kirkpatrick** | EXO's evaluation standard for engagements, New World form: Level 4 result and baseline agreed before delivery, Level 3 evidenced on scored call reviews at 30/90 days, Level 4 reported as contribution with confounders named. | Durable taxonomy and shared language; **not** a validated causal model — reaction↔learning correlation is r ≈ .07–.08, so each level is evidenced separately. |

**How they interlock:** SPICED runs the conversation, MEDDPICC inspects the deal, BANT triages the first touch, Challenger supplies the point of view. GROW structures the coaching conversation about all of it; Kolb structures the practice; 70:20:10 reminds everyone where the development actually happens; Kirkpatrick holds EXO to proving it. Underneath all of it sits the Learning Design Playbook (Dirksen): diagnose the gap first, set an honest destination, design the follow-up before the session. The frameworks serve that sequence. None of them replaces it.

## Use with explicit caution — EXO's standing flags

1. **70:20:10 — caution: folklore risk.** The most misquoted model in L&D. EXO uses it only as a labelled heuristic, never with the percentages presented as research findings, and never to justify cutting formal training — for new SDRs the formal share is deliberately far above 10%. EXO's own published content debunks the ratio-as-fact; internal use must stay consistent with that or not happen.
2. **Kolb learning styles — do not use at all.** The cycle is in; the styles are out. Style-matching failed the defining evidence review (Pashler et al., 2008) and the LSI's reliability/validity record is poor (Coffield et al., 2004). No rep gets labelled, no delivery gets style-matched. If a client asks for it, EXO explains why not — that explanation is a credibility asset.
3. **Challenger — caution: context and seniority.** Built on senior reps in complex, recession-era solution sales; never independently replicated; Gartner itself moved on. Taught to SDRs only as behaviours at the right stage, never as scripted provocation aimed at buyers who know more than the rep. No real insight asset, no Challenger training — EXO builds the point of view first.
4. **BANT — caution: buyer experience.** Seller-centric by origin and interrogation-shaped in folklore form. In EXO it is demoted to internal shorthand, taught need-led, and explicitly taught *against* as a disqualification gate — because EXO's buyers (SDR leaders inside buying groups) are exactly the people hard-gate BANT would wrongly kill.
5. **MEDDPICC — caution: overhead and theatre.** Right tool for complex deals; a tax on velocity motions and a CRM form-filling exercise when implemented as fields instead of evidence standards. EXO keeps it out of SDR metrics entirely.
6. **GROW — caution: not a teaching substitute.** A structure for drawing out capability that exists. When the diagnosis says knowledge or skills gap, EXO teaches directly first. GROW run ceremonially over a rep who needs instruction is the failure mode — and it is never used to launder a predetermined answer; if the answer must be X, EXO says so directly. That is what "direct feedback, including the hard conversations" means.

## What EXO does not claim

- That any named methodology is proven to raise win rates. None of them carries that evidence, and EXO won't pretend otherwise in a sales conversation.
- That Nick ran any specific named deal under any specific named methodology. The claim is experience of these methods in live deals across a carrying-the-number career — stated at that level, because that is the level the record supports.
- That a framework, once installed, fixes a team. Frameworks don't. Diagnosis, practice, feedback and follow-up do — the frameworks just give that work a shared language.
