# Worked Example — Week One SDR Onboarding Workshop + Companion Module

**Built with the EXO Learning Design Playbook** (gap diagnosis → destination → arc → practice → feedback → environment → follow-up). This is the playbook proven on a real deliverable: the Week One stage of EXO's onboarding journey (Day One → **Week One** → Month Three → Promotion).

The situation: a cohort of brand-new SDRs, end of their first week. Day One covered welcome, company story, product overview, tools logins. They have enthusiasm, almost no structure, and their first live calls are coming in Week Two. This workshop is the bridge: by the end of it they can run the skeleton of a real outbound call, with support, and they know exactly what happens next in their development.

---

## PART A — The Workshop Blueprint, filled in

```
WORKSHOP SESSION BLUEPRINT — EXO Enablement

Session title:  Week One: Run the Call — Structure, ICP Fluency & Discovery Basics
Client / cohort: New SDR cohort (template — set per client)
Stage: Week One
Duration: 3 hours (09:30–12:30), one break
Facilitator: EXO coach
```

### 1. The problem

New SDRs start live calling in Week Two. Without a call structure, first calls default to pitching: feature-dump, no agenda, no discovery, call over in 90 seconds. Managers then spend Month One un-teaching habits that set in during the first fifty calls. Evidence: this is the pattern in every client's early-call recordings we've reviewed at diagnostic — the problem is structural, not effort.

### 2. Gap diagnosis

- **Knowledge — YES (secondary).** They can't yet describe the ICP in buyer terms (who, what problem, what trigger), and discovery question types are new.
- **Skills — YES (PRIMARY).** Call structure and live questioning cannot be proficient without practice — nobody has ever talked their way to call fluency. They have had zero reps with feedback.
- **Motivation — partial.** High enthusiasm, but anxiety about sounding stupid on calls and in front of peers. Design must protect confidence while stretching them.
- **Habit — not yet.** No habits exist to build on or unlearn — which is precisely why Week One matters: the first habits form here whether we design them or not.
- **Environment — flagged to client.** Pre-work: the talk track must live inside the dialler/sequencer before this session, and call recordings must be on. If either is missing, that's the client's fix, agreed before delivery — training reps on a framework they can't access at the desk is theatre.
- **Communication — checked.** "Good call" must mean the same thing to the SDRs, their manager and us: the rubric in §7 below is shared with the manager in advance and used in their 1:1s from Week Two.

### 3. The learners

- 4–8 brand-new SDRs; some with adjacent experience (retail, recruitment, support), most with none in outbound B2B.
- Prior knowledge: Day One product/company overview; companion e-learning module (Part B) completed on Day 3–4 — so ICP basics and the call map arrive pre-loaded.
- Misconceptions they bring: "discovery = asking lots of questions"; "a good call ends in a meeting at any cost"; "objections mean I'm failing"; "top reps wing it."
- Context pressures: first live calls next week; new team watching; manager's activity dashboard already counting.
- Stupid-test risks: cold role-play in front of the whole room on day five. Removed — all first reps are in pairs, then trios; nobody performs solo to the room in Week One.

### 4. The destination

**Objectives** (each passes both tests — real-world behaviour, observable):

1. Given an account brief, state who the buyer is, the problem we solve for them, and a plausible trigger — in the buyer's language, not product language.
2. Run the opening 90 seconds of an outbound call: permission/agenda frame, relevance statement tied to the trigger, and a bridge into discovery — following the call map, with the talk track available.
3. In a simulated call, ask at least three diagnostic discovery questions that build on the prospect's answers (not a scripted list read in order), before any product mention.
4. Close a simulated call with a specific next step (named time, named people) or a clean, respectful disqualify.

**Sophistication target:** Apply (run the structure in live simulations), with Analyse beginning (hear the difference between a diagnostic question and an interrogation).

**Proficiency target — stated honestly to the client:** **Conscious Action** on call structure (right, with effort, using the talk track) and **Conscious Effort** on discovery questioning. Not proficiency. Fluency is a Month Three outcome built from live reps + coaching, and this workshop is the start of that, not a substitute for it.

**Fast layer (teach directly):** the call map (5 stages), ICP snapshot, talk-track locations in the tools, the three question types.
**Slow layer (conditions only):** listening, curiosity, handling silence, judgement about when to push. We start these with examples and reps; we do not promise them by Friday.

### 5. The arc (3 hours — practice ≈ 55%)

| Time | Segment | Mode | What learners DO | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 09:30–09:45 | The call that died | Input (story) | Listen to a real anonymised 90-second call that collapses; call out where it died | Elephant hook, intrinsic: their Week Two fear, named safely. Sets up everything |
| 09:45–10:00 | The call map | Input | Get the one-page call map (5 stages); annotate where the dead call broke down | Shelves before stuff — the map organises the whole day and the whole job |
| 10:00–10:20 | ICP fluency drill | Practice (pairs) | Account-brief cards: in pairs, 3 minutes per card — who, problem, trigger, in buyer language; partner checks against the ICP snapshot | Retrieval of the pre-work module, applied. Coach circulates, corrects product-speak |
| 10:20–10:40 | Openings: good vs bad | Input + analysis | Hear two openings (counter-example pair); in trios, name what the good one did, in call-map terms | Counter-examples pin the boundary. Builds the rubric language they'll be coached against |
| 10:40–11:00 | Opening reps | Practice (pairs) | Each rep runs the 90-second opening 3× rotating accounts; partner scores against a 4-line checklist | Scaffolded: talk track open in front of them. Short reps, immediate retry |
| 11:00–11:10 | Break | — | — | Built-in rest, not stolen rest |
| 11:10–11:30 | Discovery: questions that earn answers | Input | The three question types (situation, problem, consequence) taught through one annotated real call — paused at the moments that matter | Show, don't tell. Explicitly kills the "discovery = interrogation" misconception |
| 11:30–12:05 | Full-call simulations | Practice (trios) | Rep / prospect / observer rotation. Prospect cards carry a hidden problem and one objection. Observer scores the full rubric | The core of the session. Coach samples each trio, corrects in the moment. Talk track allowed; supports fade in round 3 (map only) |
| 12:05–12:20 | Playback | Feedback | One simulation (volunteer trio) replayed/discussed against the rubric; group names strengths first, then the one change | Consequence feedback: "hear where the prospect cooled — what happened just before?" |
| 12:20–12:30 | Close: habits & what's next | Commitment | Each rep writes two if-then plans; coach sets out Week Two (first live calls, call-of-the-day, coaching cadence) | See §8–9 |

### 6. Practice plan

- **Tasks are the actual task:** real account types from the client's ICP, realistic prospect cards (busy ops manager, sceptical champion, gatekeeper brush-off), the client's real opening objection set.
- **Scaffolding:** Round 1 — full talk track in hand. Round 2 — call map + talk track. Round 3 — call map only. Nobody is pushed past their support level in Week One; the fade is the design.
- **Variety:** three prospect personas, two objection types, one disqualify scenario (a clean "no" is a successful rep, and they need to have practised one before a real prospect teaches them the hard way).
- **Grouping:** pairs for low-stakes reps, trios for simulations (the observer role is learning too — scoring against the rubric builds the ear they'll use on their own calls).

### 7. Feedback plan

- **Criteria:** the EXO Week One call rubric — 8 lines across opening, discovery, and close (the same rubric the manager uses in 1:1s and we use in Month One coaching; one standard, heard everywhere).
- **Channels:** partner checklist (openings), observer rubric scores (simulations), coach correction in the moment, group playback analysis.
- **Frequency:** after every rep — reps are short precisely so feedback can be immediate and the retry instant.
- **Protection rule:** feedback is on the call, never the person; strengths named first from the recording, one change at a time. Anything personal is a private word, never a room moment.

### 8. Environment supports after the session

- **Job aids in the world:** the one-page call map and talk track live inside the dialler/sequencer (agreed with the client pre-session); printed call map for the desk in week one only — training wheels, deliberately temporary.
- **Prompts in the workflow:** the sequencer's first-call step carries the three question types as a prompt; the CRM call-log form requires outcome + one-line "what I learned" — recognition over recall.
- **Habit being built:** *log before you dial* — shrunk version: outcome + one line, nothing more, chained to the end of every call. If-then plans written in the close, e.g. *"If a prospect says 'just send me information', then I ask one question — 'happy to; so I send the right thing, what's the main thing you're trying to fix?'"*
- **Obstacles the client removes:** none blocking for Week One in the standard diagnostic; the recurring one — activity dashboards that punish longer discovery calls — is raised at Month Three design, in writing, if present.

### 9. Follow-up (scheduled before the session runs)

- **Week Two:** first live calls; reps bring one recording each to the call-of-the-day huddle (15 min, peer + coach, rubric language only).
- **Week Three:** coach reviews two recorded calls per rep against the rubric; feedback in coaching session; module refresher drops (Part B spacing plan).
- **Manager reinforcement:** manager listens to one call per rep per week using the same rubric; Week One objectives are the 1:1 agenda for the first month.
- **Month Three stage** picks up discovery depth and objection fluency from exactly where this leaves off.

### 10. Evaluation

- Behaviour: % of sampled Week Three calls that follow the call-map structure and include ≥3 diagnostic questions before a product mention (rubric-scored sample, compared against the client's pre-EXO baseline calls from diagnostic).
- Success case / struggle case: the highest- and lowest-scoring reps at Week Three get a short case review — what differed (reps completed? manager reinforcement? prior experience?) — which feeds the Month Three design.
- Not measured: attendance, happiness scores, quiz results.

## PART B — Companion e-learning module, blueprint filled in

This module is the workshop's pre-work: it loads the shelves (ICP + call map) so workshop time goes on practice, not presentation. Its spacing plan then carries the reinforcement after the session.

```
E-LEARNING MODULE BLUEPRINT — EXO Enablement

Module title:  Know Your Buyer: ICP Fluency & The Call Map
Series / stage: EXO Onboarding — Week One (pre-work for "Run the Call")
Position: Taken Day 3–4, before the Week One workshop
Target length: 9 minutes
```

**1. Gap it serves.** Knowledge gap (ICP facts, call-map structure) plus fast-layer familiarity. It does not attempt the skills gap — that's the workshop's job, and the module says so in its intro, so nobody mistakes watching for ability.

**2. The learner.** Day 3–4 SDR. Assumes Day One product overview only. Taken at their desk, between shadowing sessions — designed for one sitting, phone-readable, no audio dependency (captions on all clips).

**3. Objective (one).** *Given a one-paragraph account brief, the learner can state the buyer, their problem, and a plausible trigger in buyer language — and can place any call moment on the five-stage call map.* Proficiency target: Comprehension moving to Conscious Effort — the workshop takes it to Conscious Action.

**4. Structure.**

- **Opens with a challenge, not content (90 sec):** an account brief appears — *"You're calling the Head of Operations at a 200-person logistics firm. What do you actually know about her world?"* Learner picks from three relevance statements: one in buyer language, two in product-speak. Consequence shown immediately: the prospect's likely reaction to each.
- **Chunk 1 — The ICP snapshot (2 min):** who we sell to, the three problems we solve, what a trigger event looks like. One page, buyer's words, with the product-speak version crossed out beside it (counter-example built in).
- **Chunk 2 — The call map (2 min):** the five stages, one line each, each illustrated by a 15-second real call clip (anonymised) — the stage heard, not described.
- **Chunk 3 — Spot the stage (2 min):** three short call excerpts; learner places each on the map and says what should happen next.
- **Close (90 sec):** "Your workshop on Friday is where this becomes real calls. Bring one question about a stage you can't yet hear." — sets up the live session and surfaces confusion early.

**5. Retrieval & practice.** Every chunk ends in retrieval, not review: recall the three problems from memory (type them, then compare), place the excerpts, choose the buyer-language statement. No "click next" content screens. Real-world task on completion: *shadow one live call before the workshop and note where it moved between stages — bring the note.*

**6. Spacing plan.** First taken Day 3–4. Re-surfaced: a 3-question drip at Day 10 (ICP from memory, one spot-the-stage clip), a harder version at Week 3 alongside call reviews, and the spot-the-stage set reappears in the Month Three pre-work with messier calls. The portal issues the prompts; the learner's self-rating against the call rubric is attached at Week 3.

**7. Connection to live work.** Direct pre-work for the Week One workshop (the ICP drill and openings assume it). Feeds the Week Two call-of-the-day huddle. Manager sees completion and the drip results in aggregate only — this is preparation data, not a performance judgement, and it's presented to the client that way.

**8. Pilot.** Test on the first two reps of any new cohort before the rest are assigned: watch for where the account-brief challenge confuses (usually trigger vs problem — if pilots mix them up, Chunk 1 gets a worked example before rollout).

## Why this example is built the way it is — the Dirksen logic, briefly

- **Gap first:** the presenting problem ("new reps pitch too much") is primarily a *skills* gap with a knowledge underlay — so the design spends most of its money on reps and feedback, and pushes the knowledge into a 9-minute module instead of burning workshop time on slides.
- **Shelves before stuff:** the module and the call map give novices an organising structure before any detail lands.
- **Honest destination:** Conscious Action, stated to the client. Anyone promising fluent SDRs after one week is selling something.
- **Nobody feels stupid:** pairs before trios, volunteers for playback, feedback on the call not the person — in week one, confidence *is* a learning outcome.
- **The session is not the intervention:** job aids in the dialler, a habit chained to every call, a manager reinforcing with the same rubric, and follow-up already in the diary. That's where Week One actually works or doesn't.
